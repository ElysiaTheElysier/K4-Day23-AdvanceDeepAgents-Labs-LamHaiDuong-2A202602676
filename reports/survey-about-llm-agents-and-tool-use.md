# Survey about LLM Agents and Tool Use

## Executive Summary (TL;DR)
This survey explores the multifaceted domain of Large Language Model (LLM) agents, focusing on their integration with tools, core architectures, recent advancements, and the challenges they face. By critically examining various studies and methodologies, we highlight the progress and gaps in the current understanding of LLM agents and their tool usage, providing insights into future avenues for research.

## Background and Problem Formulation
Large Language Model agents have become increasingly significant across various applications, particularly in natural language processing and automation. These agents are defined as systems that employ LLMs to perform tasks, often leveraging external tools to enhance their capabilities. The historical evolution of LLM agents has seen significant advancements—from early models that struggled with simple tasks to present-day agents capable of complex reasoning and tool interaction. Key frameworks like AvaTaR utilize contrastive reasoning to optimize agent performance, while benchmarks such as InfoMosaic-Bench enhance their capability to integrate diverse tools in multi-source information seeking [1]. 

## Core Architectures and Methodologies
The architecture of LLM agents is pivotal to their functionalities. For instance, the dual-stream CrossDiT architecture allows for synchronized audio and video generation, showcasing a significant leap in multi-modal capabilities [2]. Additionally, methods like MiMo-V2.6 emphasize the hybrid-SWA architecture's role in improving reinforcement learning strategies for self-improvement [3]. These innovations underline the importance of architecture in facilitating effective tool use, where agents can dynamically react to complex environments and user needs.

## Recent Advances and State-of-the-Art
Recent studies have demonstrated that LLM agents are evolving towards more proactive functionalities. Frameworks aimed at enhancing memory management and decision-making capabilities have emerged, driving forward the field of autonomous agents. For example, the AGILE framework significantly improves conversational responses by leveraging memory and external tool interactions [4]. Additionally, the introduction of benchmarks tailored for evaluating LLM capabilities across various tasks such as temporal reasoning reflects an ongoing effort to refine agent performance [5]. These advancements contribute to the realization of sophisticated personal agents capable of managing complex information tasks across different domains.

## Trends, Open Challenges, and Future Directions
Despite the progress made, several challenges remain for LLM agents. Ethical concerns regarding their use in sensitive topics like healthcare highlight the need for responsible model development [6]. Furthermore, liability issues within agent systems pose significant governance challenges [7]. Research must focus on developing robust evaluation frameworks and addressing ethical considerations to ensure LLM agents operate within safe and effective parameters. The future of LLM agents lies in their ability to integrate human-like reasoning and emotional understanding, particularly in subjective language contexts [8]. Addressing these gaps is crucial for advancing the reliability and effectiveness of LLM agents.
